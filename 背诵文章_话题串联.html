Escape from the Daily Grind逃离朝九晚五 · 记叙文 · 约420词

01 工作02 压力11 休闲 15 假期16 天气08 交通

My cousin Maya used to earn big bucks as a software engineer at a tech giant. On paper, she had it all: she had been promoted twice, got a raise almost every year, and everyone assumed she would climb the corporate ladder and become the top dog of her department one day. But behind the impressive title, she was just another workaholic trapped in the rat race. 我的表姐玛雅曾是某科技巨头的软件工程师，赚着一大笔钱。纸面上，她什么都有：升过两次职，几乎年年涨薪，大家都以为她会一路爬上职场阶梯，有朝一日成为部门的一把手。但在光鲜的头衔背后，她不过是又一个困在激烈竞争里的工作狂。

Her nine-to-five job was never really nine to five. She worked overtime almost every evening, ate ready-to-eat meals at her desk, and kept answering emails on her laptop until midnight. Her workload was enormous. Whenever a deadline approached, she grew tense and anxious; when several deadlines piled up, she became truly stressed out; and by the end of the quarter she felt overwhelmed and completely burned out. "I'm making a living," she told me, "but I'm not really living." 她那份朝九晚五的工作从来就不是真正的朝九晚五。她几乎每晚加班，在工位上吃即食食品，直到半夜还在笔记本电脑上回邮件。她的工作量巨大：截止日期临近，她就变得紧绷焦虑；几个截止日期堆在一起，她就真的压力爆表；到了季度末，她整个人不堪重负、彻底累垮。"我是在谋生，"她告诉我，"但我并没有真正生活。"

The warning signs were impossible to ignore. She couldn't fall asleep, she snapped at her colleagues, and even a quiet walk no longer helped her relieve stress. Her doctor was blunt: "You are under too much pressure. Take some time off before your body files the complaint for you." So Maya applied for two weeks' leave, and for the first time in years she allowed herself to dream about a real family holiday — not a weekend staycation at home, but a proper trip to the sea. 警告信号已无法忽视：她睡不着觉，动辄冲同事发火，连安静的散步也无法再帮她缓解压力。医生说得很直白："你压力太大了。趁身体还没替你'投诉'，赶紧休段时间假吧。"于是玛雅申请了两周的假，多年来第一次让自己梦想一场真正的家庭度假——不是周末在家宅度假，而是一场像样的海边之旅。

Escaping the city, of course, had its own price. She left home in the middle of rush hour, and the traffic was already bumper-to-bumper; a minor accident on the ring road had turned the morning commute into total gridlock. She finally reached the airport, exhausted — only to hear that freezing weather had stranded hundreds of passengers overnight. But when her plane finally took off, the clouds began to clear up. She landed in the sunny south, where the air was mild, a light drizzle hung over the mountains, and a soft breeze followed her all the way to the hotel. 当然，逃离城市也有它的代价。她在高峰时段出门，路上早已车挨着车；环线上一场小事故把早高峰通勤变成了彻底的僵局。她疲惫地赶到机场，却听到广播说严寒天气已让数百名乘客滞留过夜。不过当飞机终于起飞，云层渐渐放晴。她降落在阳光明媚的南方：那里空气温和，山间飘着毛毛细雨，一路伴随她到酒店的，还有轻柔的微风。

That trip changed her. She swam in the sea, hiked in the hills, and took a nap every afternoon. She learned to unwind without feeling guilty, and discovered that laughing with strangers during happy hour could blow off steam better than any app. Regular exercise revitalized her body; slow, unhurried mornings recharged her batteries. By the time she flew home, she felt entirely stress-free. 那次旅行改变了她。她下海游泳、上山徒步，每天午后小睡一会儿。她学会了不带负罪感地放松，还发现和陌生人在酒吧特价时段大笑一场，比任何软件都能发泄减压。规律运动让她的身体恢复了活力；慢悠悠的清晨给她重新充了电。等飞回家时，她整个人毫无压力，焕然一新。

Nowadays Maya works as a freelancer. Last winter, when the company laid off half her department, her former colleagues escaped through job-hopping; Maya chose a third road and quit for good. She earns much less now — her old teammates tease her that she used to make big bucks and now works for chicken feed — but she finally has a real work-life balance. She has time to hang out with friends, to binge-watch her favorite shows, to enjoy proper me time, and to visit our uncle, who retired last spring and now teaches meditation in the park. "Working too much might make you wealthy," she says, "but balance gives you the greatest wealth of all — good health. No promotion is worth burning out for." 如今玛雅做自由职业。去年冬天公司裁掉了她部门一半的人，前同事们纷纷靠跳槽脱身，玛雅则选了第三条路：彻底辞职。她现在挣得少多了——老同事打趣说，她从赚大钱变成了挣"鸡食钱"——但她终于拥有了真正的工作与生活的平衡。她有时间和朋友出去玩，追喜欢的剧，享受属于自己的时间，还能去看望去年春天退休、如今在公园教冥想的舅舅。"工作太多也许让人富有，"她说，"但平衡给你最大的财富——健康。任何升职都不值得把自己累垮。"

Will she ever go back to the daily grind of corporate life? "Never say never," she laughed when I asked. "But if I do, it will be on my terms. When the right chance comes along, I'll grab the opportunity — after checking, of course, that it won't bury me in deadlines again. Until then, I'm taking it easy. Life is too short to spend it stressed out." 她还会回到职场的日常苦差中去吗？"永远别说永远，"被问到时她笑了，"但如果回去，那也得按我的条件来。合适的机会一旦出现，我会抓住它——当然，得先确认它不会再次把我埋进截止日期堆里。在那之前，我悠着点来。人生苦短，别把时间都耗在焦虑崩溃上。"

A Healthier Me更健康的我 · 第一人称记叙 · 约480词

10 习惯04 饮食03 外食 05 另类疗法17 园艺

At last year's health check, my doctor looked at me over her glasses and gave me a friendly warning: I had put on weight, my blood pressure was creeping up, and years of poor nutrition were starting to show. Like many office workers, I had slipped into one bad habit after another — and as the saying goes, bad habits die hard. 去年体检时，医生扶了扶眼镜，友好地警告我：我长胖了，血压在悄悄攀升，多年糟糕的营养状况开始显形。像很多上班族一样，我一个个地滑进了坏习惯里——俗话说得好，旧习难改。

Let me be honest about my old routine. I had a habit of skipping breakfast, grabbing a bite of junk food at noon, and comforting myself with comfort food at midnight. I lived on ready-to-eat meals and processed food — salty snacks, sugary drinks, whatever was fastest. Whenever a craving struck, I simply gave in to it; my friends joked that I could eat like a horse at midnight yet eat like a bird at proper meals. Not surprisingly, I kept putting on weight, and my metabolism seemed to slow down every year. 坦白说说我的旧日常吧：我有不吃早餐的习惯，中午随便扒两口垃圾食品，深夜再用"治愈系美食"安慰自己。我靠即食食品和加工食品过活——咸零食、含糖饮料，什么快吃什么。食欲一来我就缴械投降；朋友们笑我半夜能"吃得像匹马"，正经吃饭却"吃得像只鸟"。不出所料，我体重一路上涨，新陈代谢仿佛一年慢过一年。

So I made up my mind to break those habits for good. I didn't quit cold turkey — small steps last longer — but I cut down on sugar and salt, gave up fried food entirely, and got into the habit of cooking for myself. Within a month, a balanced diet full of vegetables and fruit, plus plenty of water, had become part of my daily routine; three months later it felt like second nature. It took real willpower at the beginning, but I stuck to it, and in the long run the healthy choice became the easy one. 于是我下定决心，永远戒掉这些习惯。我没有"火鸡式"骤戒——小步子才走得远——但我减少了糖和盐的摄入，彻底告别油炸食品，养成了自己做饭的习惯。不出一个月，充满蔬果的均衡饮食加上充足的饮水，就成了我日常的一部分；三个月后，这简直成了我的第二天性。起初确实需要意志力，但我坚持了下来——长远看，健康的选择变成了容易的选择。

Eating out was my biggest obstacle, because my friends and I all love restaurants. But we have learned to order wisely. We book a table in advance instead of queuing; we never order too much, because we hate to waste food; and although my boyfriend loves announcing "My treat!", we usually go Dutch and split the bill to keep things fair. When the service is excellent, we leave a generous tip; when we are too tired to go out, we simply order takeaway. Yes, I still have a sweet tooth — but nowadays I satisfy it with fruit instead of cake. Little by little, eating well stopped being a diet and became a lifestyle. 出去吃饭是我最大的障碍，因为我和朋友们都是餐馆爱好者。但我们学会了聪明点餐：提前订位而不去排队；绝不点多，因为我们讨厌浪费食物；虽然男朋友喜欢宣布"我请客！"，我们通常还是AA制、分摊账单，这样才公平。服务出色时，我们会给丰厚的小费；太累不想出门时，就直接点外卖。没错，我仍然爱吃甜食——但如今我用水果而不是蛋糕来满足它。一点一点地，好好吃饭不再是"节食"，而成了一种生活方式。

Movement came next — and, surprisingly, so did alternative medicine. I was skeptical of it at first, wondering whether the benefits were just the placebo effect. But my grandmother swears by herbal remedies for colds, so I kept an open mind. Now I practice yoga every morning to keep my body and mind in equilibrium; I go for a massage once a month to relieve stress; and I have even tried acupuncture for my stiff shoulder. Unlike many modern drugs, these gentle therapies have almost no side effects, and they seem to alleviate my chronic neck pain better than painkillers. My doctor approves of all this, by the way. As she likes to say, "Prevention is better than cure — and getting enough sleep is the best medicine." 运动是第二步——出乎意料的是，另类疗法也来了。起初我对它持怀疑态度，怀疑那些效果不过是安慰剂效应。但我祖母对治感冒的草药疗法深信不疑，于是我保持着开放的心态。现在，我每天早晨练瑜伽，让身心保持平衡；每月去做一次按摩缓解压力；甚至为僵硬的肩膀试过针灸。和许多现代药物不同，这些温和的疗法几乎没有副作用，而且缓解我的慢性脖子疼比止痛药还管用。顺便说一句，医生很赞成这些。正如她爱说的："预防胜于治疗——而充足的睡眠是最好的药。"

The happiest discovery, though, was gardening. Last spring I started growing my own vegetables on the balcony — tomatoes, peppers, and a pot of strawberries. At first it was a constant battle against weeds; I had to learn when to water the plants, how to prune the tomatoes, and how to add fertilizer to the soil without burning the seedlings. My grandfather, whose flowerbed is the pride of the whole street, still mows the lawn every Sunday and trims the hedge twice a summer. My friends say I have a green thumb; honestly, anyone can do it. Gardening is therapeutic and deeply rewarding — watching a seedling push through the soil feels beyond belief, and it revitalizes me more than any supplement could. 不过最令人欣喜的发现是园艺。去年春天，我开始在自家阳台种菜——番茄、辣椒，还有一盆草莓。起初这是一场与杂草的持久战；我得学会什么时候浇水、怎么给番茄修枝、怎么往土里施肥又不烧坏幼苗。我祖父的花坛是整条街的骄傲，他至今每周日割草坪，每夏天修剪两次树篱。朋友们说我有"绿手指"（园艺天赋）；说实话，谁都能做到。园艺很治愈，也极有成就感——看着幼苗破土而出简直难以置信，它比任何保健品都更能让我恢复活力。

One year later, I have lost a lot of weight, my doctor is delighted, and I feel stronger than I have in years. My secret is not a strict dietary regimen, and it is certainly not magic pills. It is simply this: good habits, good nourishment, daily movement — and a small patch of green of my own. Health, it turns out, is not something you buy. It is something you grow. 一年后的今天，我瘦了很多，医生很高兴，我也觉得多年来从未如此有活力。我的秘诀不是严格的饮食养生法，更不是什么神奇药丸，只有这几样：好习惯、好的滋养、每天动一动——外加一小片属于自己的绿色。事实证明，健康不是买来的，而是种出来的。

The Bookworm Who Learned to Speak学会开口的书虫 · 成长记叙 · 约530词

12 性格13 阅读25 恐惧 23 幽默14 梦想目标

If you had met me in college, you would have seen a typical bookworm: quiet, introverted, and more than a little reserved. My disposition was sweet but shy — or so my friends kindly put it. I was happiest alone in the library with a page-turner; at parties I hid in the corner; in class I never raised my hand. I was the opposite of a people person. And as for speaking in public — just the thought of it gave me butterflies in my stomach. 如果你见过大学时代的我，会看到一个典型的书虫：安静、内向，还相当闷。用朋友们客气的话说，我"性情温和但害羞"。独自在图书馆里捧着一本让人爱不释手的书，是我最快乐的时刻；派对上我躲在角落，课堂上从不举手——妥妥的社交达人的反面。至于公开演讲——光是想想，我心里就发慌。

Books were my refuge. I could devour three novels a week, and once I started a good thriller, I simply couldn't put it down. I loved losing myself in a book on rainy afternoons, following every twist of the plot. Reading broadened my horizons and enriched my vocabulary — but it was a private joy. I knew my favorite characters' strengths and weaknesses by heart, yet I had no idea how to talk to actual people. 书是我的避难所。我一周能如饥似渴地读完三本小说，一旦翻开一本好看的惊悚小说，就根本放不下来。雨天午后，我最爱沉浸在书里，追随情节的每一次转折。阅读开阔了我的视野、丰富了我的词汇——但那只是一个人的快乐。我能背出最爱角色的种种优缺点，却不知道该怎么和真人交谈。

Then, in my final year, my dream job appeared on a company website, and I daydreamed about it for weeks. But there was an obstacle I could not ignore: the final interview required a ten-minute speech in English. For someone with a phobia of public speaking, that felt like being asked to jump off a cliff. Whenever I imagined the interview, my fear was heightened: my heart raced, my hands got sweaty, and I would simply freeze. I was petrified — and, worse, tempted to give up before even trying. 大四那年，我梦想的工作出现在公司网站上，我为此做了好几周的白日梦。但有一个无法忽视的障碍：终面要求用英语做十分钟演讲。对一个有演讲恐惧症的人来说，这简直像被要求跳崖。每当想象面试现场，我的恐惧就会被放大：心跳加速、手心出汗、整个人僵在原地。我吓僵了——更糟的是，我还没尝试就想放弃。

My roommate Lily refused to let me. She is outgoing and easygoing, gets along well with almost everyone, and has a fabulous sense of humor — in short, everything I was not. "Don't judge a person's character by how loud they are," she said, "and don't let fear get in the way of your dream. Getting out of your comfort zone is the first step to growth. What is the greatest invention of all time? Ask ten people and you will get ten different answers: the printing press, electricity, antibiotics. Most experts, however, agree on two strong candidates — the computer and the Internet, two of the greatest inventions of the modern world. Together they have broken new ground in almost every field and changed the game for how we live, learn, and work. 有史以来最伟大的发明是什么？问十个人，你会得到十种答案：印刷机、电、抗生素。不过多数专家对两个热门候选者意见一致——计算机和互联网，现代世界最伟大的两项发明。它们几乎在每个领域都开了新局，彻底改变了我们生活、学习和工作的格局。

Think about how much daily life has shifted. Twenty years ago, offices printed hard copies of every report and requested a printout of every phone bill; today we email a soft copy and sign on a screen. My first computer was a heavy desktop with a noisy monitor, a sticky keyboard, and a mouse that froze twice a day. Now a thin laptop, a tablet and a pair of comfortable headsets share my desk. We surf the internet, stream films, download files, bookmark useful pages, and back everything up to the cloud — so that even if the computer crashes or the screen suddenly freezes, our work survives. When a glitch appears, we restart, update the software, and carry on. 想想日常生活变了多少吧。二十年前，办公室里每份报告都要打印纸质版，每份话费账单都要申请打印件；今天我们发个电子版、在屏幕上签字就完事了。我的第一台电脑是笨重的台式机：显示器吵、键盘发涩、鼠标一天冻住两回。现在，一台轻薄笔记本、一块平板和一副舒适的耳机共享我的桌面。我们上网冲浪、看电影、下载文件、收藏有用的网页，把一切都备份到云端——这样一来，就算电脑死机、屏幕突然卡死，工作也丢不了。出了小故障，重启一下、更新软件，继续干活。

And the pace never slows. This year's flagship phone "has the best features so far" — a state-of-the-art camera, a faster chip, an upgrade nobody strictly needs but everybody wants. Tech giants race to launch smart-home systems, delivery drones, and clever little gadgets of every size. AI is reshaping entire industries; virtual reality is no longer science fiction — it is here. Autonomous vehicles are said to be ideal for inexperienced drivers, although surveys show that many people still refuse to trust a driverless car. There is a shadow side, and it fits in your pocket. Studies reveal that people now spend around two hours a day on social media — and many of us spend far more. I still remember the winters of my childhood, when our city suffered from heavy smog and the sky was the color of dishwater. The causes are no secret either. Pollution, in short, is harmful material living where it does not belong — and we were producing it at record speed. 原因也早已不是秘密。燃烧化石燃料向空气中排放温室气体；汽车尾气呛满主干道；我家乡的河曾被上游工厂的废水污染；农民滥用杀虫剂，损害了土壤；深夜施工的噪音污染则抢走了整个社区的睡眠。多年来，这座城市先处理垃圾、后提问题。简言之，污染就是有害物质住进了不属于它的地方——而我们曾以创纪录的速度制造它。

Then things began to change, from the top and from the bottom. Transportation is changing too, and it is one of the clearest places where innovation serves the planet. I usually commute by subway to dodge the rush-hour traffic jam; when the weather is mild, I ride a shared bike, because cycling to work is good for both your health and the environment — and it keeps my carbon footprint small. A better world, however, must be not only greener but also fairer. While some families are comfortably well-off, the wealth gap between rich and poor keeps widening. And here is where art enters the story. You may know the sad statistic: in deprived communities, a large share of residents have never been to a single arts event, not even a cinema. Three years ago, a local charity opened a free gallery in exactly such a neighborhood, and I attended its first exhibition of contemporary art. Was it a future masterpiece, or only a sketch that a young painter had created after taking inspiration from nature? It does not matter. That painting caught his eye, and for a moment it gave his hard days some light. Some works are breathtaking, some are thought-provoking; the best ones are simply inspiring — proof that beauty does not belong to the rich alone. 而这里，正是艺术出场的地方。你可能知道那个令人难过的数据：在贫困社区，相当多的居民从未参加过一场艺术活动，甚至连电影院都没进过。三年前，一家本地慈善机构恰恰在这样的社区开了一间免费画廊，我去看了它的首场当代艺术展。我看到一位老人在一幅抽象画前站了将近二十分钟——画上全是鲜艳炽烈的色彩。它是一件未来的杰作，还是某位年轻画家从大自然汲取灵感后画下的素描？这不重要。那幅画抓住了他的目光，有一瞬间，它照亮了他艰辛的日子。有些作品令人惊艳，有些发人深省；最好的作品就是能鼓舞人心——证明美并不只属于富人。

A greener city and a kinder world: in the end they are not two dreams but one. Clean air, fair chances, art within reach — each of them starts with the same small decision, repeated daily: to care, and to do our part. Prevention is better than cure, for the planet as much as for people. The Earth is the only home we all share, and it deserves our best habits. 更绿色的城市，更温暖的世界：说到底，这不是两个梦，而是同一个。清洁的空气、公平的机会、触手可及的艺术——它们都始于同一个每天重复的小小决定：去在乎，去尽一份力。预防胜于治疗——对地球如此，对人亦然。地球是我们共同唯一的家，它值得我们拿出最好的习惯。

Home Sweet Home甜蜜的家 · 轻松记叙(Beginners友好) · 约460词

26 家电27 家具28 房间 30 社区场所09 时尚29 游乐设施

Saturday morning at our house always begins the same way. Sunlight fills the living room, and my mother is already busy in the kitchen. "Come in and have breakfast!" she calls. The washing machine hums in the corner, the refrigerator is full of fresh food, and my father, coffee in hand, is watching the morning news on television. Later he will run the vacuum cleaner — it is very noisy, but as he always says, a clean home is a happy home. I love our home: the soft sofa with its bright cushions, the big table where the four of us eat together, the quiet bathroom where my sister takes her famous bubble baths, and above all my bedroom, which I think is the most comfortable room in the world. "This bed suits your taste," my mother likes to say — and she is right. 我们家的周六早晨总是这样开始：阳光洒满客厅，妈妈已经在厨房忙开了。"进来吃早饭啦！"她喊。洗衣机在角落嗡嗡转着，冰箱里塞满新鲜食物，爸爸端着咖啡，正在电视上看早间新闻。一会儿他要开吸尘器——噪音很大，但正如他常说的：干净的家才是快乐的家。我爱我们的家：软软的沙发配着鲜艳的靠垫，我们四个人一起吃饭的大桌子，妹妹泡她那著名泡泡浴的安静浴室，还有最重要的——我的卧室，我认为它是世界上最舒服的房间。"这张床很合你的口味，"妈妈爱这么说——她说得对。

After breakfast comes the day's most serious question: what to wear? My sister loves putting together a trendy outfit; she follows every fashion trend, and her wardrobe is twice the size of mine. I am the exact opposite — I don't really follow fashion trends; I just wear what's comfortable. Still, even I know that first impressions are often based on how people dress, so for today's outing I choose something casual: jeans, a T-shirt, and white sneakers, because white sneakers go with almost everything. My sister, in a vibrant dress from a vintage shop, looks at me and sighs, "That hoodie is so out of fashion!" — "It's not old," I answer. "It's timeless." We never finish this argument, and somehow we never want to. 早饭后，迎来一天最严肃的问题：穿什么？妹妹最爱搭配时髦的穿搭，追逐每一股时尚潮流，她的衣柜是我的两倍大。我正相反——我不太追潮流，只穿舒服的。不过，就连我也知道第一印象往往取决于穿着，所以今天出门我选了休闲装：牛仔裤、T恤，加白色运动鞋——因为小白鞋几乎配一切。妹妹穿着一条从复古店淘来的鲜艳连衣裙，看了我一眼，叹气道："那件连帽衫早过时了！"——"它不是旧，"我回答，"它永不过时。"这场争论永远吵不完，但不知怎么，我们也不想吵完。

Our neighborhood is small but convenient. The grocery store is just next to our building, so we pick up fresh fruit on the way. The clinic is across the street — thankfully, today we only wave at it. My favorite place is the library: this afternoon, as usual, I will borrow some books from it. The school where my mother teaches is far from here, but on weekends the whole community slows down and feels like one big, friendly village. 我们的社区不大，但很方便。杂货店就在我们楼隔壁，顺路就能买上新鲜水果。诊所在街对面——谢天谢地，今天我们只需要跟它挥挥手。我最爱的地方是图书馆：今天下午，我会照常去借几本书。妈妈教书的学校离这儿很远，但一到周末，整个社区就慢了下来，像一个友好温暖的大村庄。

On Sunday we visit the amusement park in the suburbs. My sister runs straight to the roller coaster — "Riding the roller coaster is scary!" I shout after her, but she is already in the line. She loves the swing ride too and swears it is even scarier than the coaster; I believe her completely, which is exactly why I stay on the ground. You see, I have a phobia of heights, so I never go on the Ferris wheel — yes, even that gentle, family-friendly one. Instead, I take photos, eat ice cream, and watch the carousel turn like a slow, glittering dream. "Face your fears!" my sister yells from the sky. "Not today," I laugh. "Today I'm facing the ice cream." 周日，我们去郊区的游乐园。妹妹直奔过山车——"坐过山车很吓人的！"我在她身后喊，可她已经排上队了。她还喜欢海盗船，并发誓它比过山车更吓人；我完全相信，而这正是我待在地面的原因。你瞧，我有恐高症，所以从不坐摩天轮——对，连那种温和的、合家欢款也不坐。我呢，拍拍照、吃吃冰淇淋，看旋转木马像一场缓慢闪亮的梦转个不停。"直面你的恐惧！"妹妹从天上喊。"今天不，"我笑道，"今天我要直面的是冰淇淋。"

Home again by evening, tired and happy. The lights in the living room are warm, dinner is waiting on the table, and someone has put fresh flowers in the middle of it. Maybe our home is not the biggest or the trendiest place in the world. But when my father sinks into the sofa with a happy groan, my mother laughs from the kitchen, and my sister and I argue about whose turn it is to use the vacuum, I understand exactly what people mean by "home sweet home": it is not the appliances or the furniture. It is the people who fill the rooms with laughter. 傍晚回到家，又累又开心。客厅的灯光暖暖的，晚餐摆在桌上等着，桌子中央不知谁还放了鲜花。我们的家也许不是世界上最大、最时髦的地方。但当爸爸心满意足地陷进沙发，妈妈在厨房里笑出声，我和妹妹为"今天谁用吸尘器"争执不下时，我完全明白了人们说"甜蜜的家"是什么意思：家不在家电与家具——家是那些让每个房间充满笑声的人。
